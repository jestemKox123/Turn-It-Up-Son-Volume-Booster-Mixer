How to make any song Nightcore in your browser

You do not need a download, an editor or somebody's sped up re-upload. If the song is playing in a tab, you can Nightcore it right there in about five seconds.

The short answer

Install Turn It Up, Son!, open the tab that is already playing the song, click the extension icon, open the Mix panel and press Nightcore. The track speeds up to 1.35x, the voice rises with it, the top end gets brighter and a touch of reverb keeps it from sounding dry. That is the whole trick.

The preset does the work, so you do not have to know what any of the sliders mean. If you want to tune it afterwards, you can. If you do not, you are done.

What Nightcore actually is

Nightcore is not a genre so much as a treatment. You take a normal track, run it faster than it was recorded, and the whole thing lifts: the vocal turns light and chipmunky, the hi hats sparkle, the energy goes up. It started as sped up trance edits and ended up being applied to basically everything.

The reason the voice goes up is simple physics. A sound is a wave, and pitch is how many times per second that wave repeats. Play the recording faster and every wave in it repeats more often, so every note lands higher. Tempo and pitch are welded together unless something deliberately pulls them apart.

Modern players often do pull them apart. When you watch a video at 1.5x, the software stretches the audio so the speaker still sounds normal. That is great for lectures and useless for Nightcore. The Nightcore preset lets pitch follow tempo on purpose, which is exactly the sound you came for.

Step by step

  1. Add Turn It Up, Son! from the Chrome Web Store. It is free and it also runs in Edge and Brave.
  2. Open the song where you normally listen: YouTube, YouTube Music, Spotify, SoundCloud, anywhere that plays sound. Press play first, so the extension has something to work with.
  3. Click the extension icon in the toolbar.
  4. Open the Mix panel and pick Nightcore. The first time you use a speed effect on a site, the browser asks for permission for that one site. Accept it.
  5. Listen. If it is close but not quite there, flip on Full mixer and nudge Speed. The range runs from 0.5x up to 1.6x, and anything between 1.3x and 1.45x usually sits in the sweet spot.
  6. Found a version you love? Save it under your own name in Your mixes so you can drop it on any track later.

Tuning it to your taste

Nightcore is a starting point, not a rule. Under Full mixer you get Speed, Reverb, Brightness, Muffle, Bass, Vocal, Power and Spin, and the Bass slider comes in six flavors: Classic, Sub, Punch, Rumble, 808 and Warm.

  • Too thin? Speeding up naturally trims low end. Add a little Bass and try the Warm or Punch flavor.
  • Too harsh? Pull Brightness back. The preset sets it at 0.6, which is bright by design.
  • Want it dreamier? Push Reverb past the small 0.1 the preset uses.
  • Want the opposite? Slowed + Reverb sits in the same panel at 0.85x with reverb at 0.6.

And since this is a volume booster first, you can push the tab up to 700 percent if the source is quiet. The built in limiter catches the crackle, with a Volume ceiling, a Bass ceiling, a Maximum reduction slider, a live reduction meter and an automatic mode if you would rather not think about it.

The honest limitations

Speed is the one effect with strings attached. It works by speeding up the page's own player, which means two things. First, the page has to have a video or audio player. On a page with no player there is nothing to speed up, and Nightcore will not do anything. Second, changing speed needs permission for that specific site, and the extension hands that permission back the moment you turn the effect off.

Everything else, the boost, reverb, brightness, bass and the rest, works on any tab that makes noise.

Nothing leaves your machine

All of this runs locally. No servers, no analytics, no accounts, no telemetry. Your settings live in the browser's local extension storage and are never synced anywhere. The only two network requests that ever happen are looking up a title when you paste a song link into the Auto Skip list, and the anonymous feedback form, which is entirely voluntary.
